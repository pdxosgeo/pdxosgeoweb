Special guest Arty Rodriguez (ODFW Oregon Conservation Strategy GIS Analyst) and Ryan Hodges (Ecotrust Senior Software Developer will be talking to us about the Compass mapping application and the [http://OregonConservationStrategy.org](http://OregonConservationStrategy.org) website developed for Oregon Fish and Wildlife. 

In 2006, the Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife (ODFW) released its vision and framework for long-term fish and wildlife conservation as a 400 page .pdf. Titled the Oregon Conservation Strategy, the document included lots of text, tables, static maps, and other content, most of which were created in the early 2000's and based off of data from the late 1990's. Plant and Animal species ignored this and continued to grow, shrink, move, and change during the next 10 years anyway. In 2016 ODFW decided that if the animals and plants weren’t going to adhere to the data in strategy as written, including its myriad static maps, then the data would have to change with them.

Presented with the challenge of building a new format for the Oregon Conservation Strategy, ODFW contracted with Ecotrust to develop the [OregonConservationStrategy.org](http://OregonConservationStrategy.org) and upgrade the ODFW Compass mapping application ([http://compass.dfw.state.or.us/](http://compass.dfw.state.or.us/)). Arty Rodriguez (ODFW Oregon Conservation Strategy GIS Analyst) and Ryan Hodges (Ecotrust Senior Software Developer) will talk about the resulting websites, focusing on the map tool in particular, and the process it took to get there. Prepare to hear all about the challenges and rewards of introducing new and open source technology to government agencies; merging ESRI and open source platforms into a common solution; building websites to hold dynamic information; using online GIS tools to connect conservation priorities to on the ground actions; and, audience willing, take a deeper dive into the code.
